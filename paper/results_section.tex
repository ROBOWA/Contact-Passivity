\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

Fig.~\ref{fig:overview} summarizes the experimental system and the
implemented pipeline. All experiments share the task of
Sec.~\ref{sec:task}\footnote{Adjust this reference to your setup section.}
($F_n^d = 5$~N, $v_t^d = 0.05$~m/s), the ledger parameters of
Eqs.~(15)--(17), and the QP of Sec.~5; only the task-contact predictor, the
scripted human wrench, and the controller mode vary between tests.
Table~\ref{tab:summary} collects the summary metrics of every mode--test
combination; the following subsections discuss one implementation claim at
a time. Across all runs the QP solves in $26\text{--}48~\mu$s (median) with
sub-millisecond worst cases, i.e.\ well within the 1~kHz control budget.

\subsection{False depletion under nominal sliding (Test A)}
\label{sec:res-a}

Fig.~\ref{fig:false-depletion} shows Test~A (oracle predictor, no human).
C0, C2, and C3 are indistinguishable: $\mathrm{RMSE}(F_n)\approx0.035$~N,
$\mathrm{RMSE}(v_t)\approx0.0027$~m/s, and the C3 filter modifies the
command on only $0.009\,\%$ of in-contact steps, with both of its ledgers
exactly constant. The whole-port baseline C1, in contrast, charges the
ordinary sliding-friction dissipation ($\approx0.075$~W) to its protected
ledger: $E_W$ drains linearly and crosses its floor at $t\approx6.2$~s,
after which the scalar attenuation collapses normal-force regulation
(Fig.~\ref{fig:false-depletion}b). This is the false-passivation failure
mode that motivates contact-model subtraction: under an accurate task
model, selective passivation leaves the nominal task untouched
(claims~1--2).

\begin{figure}[t]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\figwidth]{figs/fig2_false_depletion.pdf}
  \caption{Test~A (oracle model, no human): false depletion under nominal
  sliding. (a)~The whole-port ledger $E_W$ of C1 drains on ordinary task
  friction and triggers intervention at $6.21$~s (vertical line), while the
  C3 residual and human ledgers remain exactly at their initial values.
  (b)~Normal-force and (c)~tangential-velocity tracking: C0 and C3 overlap;
  C1's post-depletion attenuation destroys force regulation.}
  \label{fig:false-depletion}
\end{figure}

\subsection{Model mismatch stays in the residual port (Test B)}
\label{sec:res-b}

With the regularized friction predictor at $\widehat{\mu}=0.20$ (true
$\mu\approx0.30$) and no human, the mismatch power
$p_\Delta\approx-(\mu-\widehat{\mu})F_n v_t\approx-25$~mW discharges only
the residual ledger (Fig.~\ref{fig:mismatch}): $E_H$ stays exactly at
$0.05$~J while $E_R$ bleeds from $0.30$~J to its floor region
($\approx0.021$~J). The first ledger-driven intervention consequently moves
from $6.21$~s (whole-port C1) to $14.73$~s (residual, C3): the predictor
postpones passivation until \emph{unexplained} contact power has consumed
the residual budget, rather than total task friction (claim~3). The
attribution identities of Sec.~3 hold to machine precision throughout
($\mathbf{w}_R=\mathbf{w}_\Delta$ here; $p_R = p_H + p_\Delta$ in every
test).

\begin{figure}[t]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\figwidth]{figs/fig3_mismatch_residual.pdf}
  \caption{Test~B (friction mismatch $\widehat{\mu}=0.20$, no human).
  (a)~The human ledger is exactly constant while the residual ledger
  depletes; first ledger-driven interventions of C1 ($6.21$~s) and C3
  ($14.73$~s) are marked. (b)~Port powers: the depletion is carried
  entirely by the mismatch power $p_\Delta$ while $p_H\equiv0$.}
  \label{fig:mismatch}
\end{figure}

\subsection{Human-directed power and energy limiting (Test C)}
\label{sec:res-c}

Fig.~\ref{fig:human-limiting} is the central quantitative comparison. The
nominal controller (C0) works against the $3$-N blocking human at
$-p_H\approx0.15$~W for the entire hold and delivers $0.361$~J. C2
(residual ledger only) behaves almost identically until its large residual
budget is exhausted: the peak human-directed power is unchanged
($0.1499$~W) and $0.280$~J are extracted, because nothing in C2
distinguishes the human from generic unexplained contact. C3 clips the
instantaneous power at the $P_H^{\max}=0.10$~W row (measured peak
$0.0997$~W), discharges $E_H$ to its floor, and saturates the cumulative
output at $0.045$~J~$=E_H(0)-E_{H,\min}$ --- an $8\times$ reduction over C0
enforced by the small dedicated budget rather than by tuning (claim~4).
After release the robot recovers nominal sliding in $\approx0.8$~s.

\begin{figure}[t]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\figwidth]{figs/fig4_human_limiting.pdf}
  \caption{Test~C (oracle model, $3$-N blocking human, shaded window):
  human-port limiting across C0/C2/C3. (a)~Instantaneous extracted power
  $-p_H$ with the $0.10$-W row; (b)~cumulative human-directed energy
  $W_H^{\mathrm{out}}$ against the $0.045$-J budget; (c)~the human ledger:
  C3 stops at $E_{H,\min}$, while the (unconstrained, observed) ledgers of

\subsection{Combined mismatch and human interaction (Test D)}
\label{sec:res-d}

Test~D repeats the blocking profile under $\widehat{\mu}=0.20$
(Fig.~\ref{fig:combined}). The human ledger reproduces Test~C exactly
($E_H\to0.005$~J, peak $0.0997$~W), while the residual ledger now pays for
both channels and ends near $0.043$~J: the stacked decomposition in
Fig.~\ref{fig:combined}b shows $-p_R$ separating into the human share
$-p_H$ (bounded by the human rows) and the persistent mismatch share
$-p_\Delta$. The nested ledgers therefore attribute energy to the correct
ports simultaneously, rather than double-counting or masking either
channel.

\begin{figure}[t]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\figwidth]{figs/fig5_combined_decomposition.pdf}
  \caption{Test~D (mismatch $\widehat{\mu}=0.20$ plus blocking human).
  (a)~Both ledgers respond: $E_H$ tracks only human power, $E_R$ tracks
  human plus mismatch. (b)~Extracted-power decomposition
  $-p_R=-p_H-p_\Delta$ (50-ms means; stacked areas sum to the black
  $-p_R$ trace).}
  \label{fig:combined}
\end{figure}

\subsection{QP authority versus scalar attenuation (Test E)}
\label{sec:res-e}

Under the oblique human force
$\mathbf{w}_H=\tfrac{3}{\sqrt2}(-\mathbf{t}-\mathbf{n})$~N, C3 and C4
enforce the same human rows, yet differ sharply in task preservation
(Fig.~\ref{fig:qp-vs-scalar}): within the interaction window
$\mathrm{RMSE}(F_n)=0.859$~N for C3 versus $2.522$~N for C4. The command
trajectories explain the gap. C3 satisfies the human rows almost entirely
with its tangential channel while holding $u_n$ on the nominal
normal-force command; C4's single scaling factor must attenuate both
channels together, so every joule of human protection is paid for with
normal-force error. This isolates the value of two-dimensional,
task-weighted projection over uniform attenuation (claim~5).

\begin{figure*}[t]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{figs/fig6_qp_vs_scalar.pdf}
  \caption{Test~E (oblique human force, shaded window): task-weighted QP
  (C3) versus scalar attenuation (C4). (a)~Normal-force regulation during
  the window ($\mathrm{RMSE}$ annotated). (b)~C3 commands: the tangential
  command is cut while the normal command follows its nominal.
  (c)~C4 commands: the scalar factor attenuates both channels, dropping
  the normal push and hence $F_n$.}
  \label{fig:qp-vs-scalar}
\end{figure*}

\subsection{Ledger charging, capping, and discharge (Test F)}
\label{sec:res-f}

Fig.~\ref{fig:charge} traces the human ledger through a helping
($+3$~N$\,\mathbf{t}$) followed by a blocking ($-3$~N$\,\mathbf{t}$)
pulse. During helping, $p_H>0$ charges $E_H$ from $0.05$~J to the
$0.08$-J cap, where charging saturates (discharge is never clamped, so the
cap is the only nonlinearity). The later blocking pulse first spends the
stored credit --- the robot resists longer than in Test~C --- and then
settles at the floor with the extraction again power-limited at
$0.10$~W. Energy received from the human genuinely extends the budget for
later interaction, while the cap bounds how much history may accumulate.

\begin{figure}[t]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\figwidth]{figs/fig7_charge_discharge.pdf}

  (a)~$E_H$ charges to the $0.08$-J cap, plateaus, and later discharges to

  bounded discharge ($p_H\geq-0.10$~W) phases.}

\subsection{Reference-governor ablation}
\label{sec:res-governor}

The time-indexed reference of Eq.~(2) keeps advancing while the human
blocks the robot; on release, the accumulated $\approx0.13$~m error
produces a catch-up burst. Fig.~\ref{fig:governor} compares C3 with the
governor disabled and enabled on the blocking scenario: the burst reaches
$0.68$~m/s and a $24$-N normal-force transient without the governor,
versus $0.050$~m/s and $5.1$~N with it --- the post-release overshoot of
$F_n$ is reduced by $\approx99\,\%$ and the velocity never exceeds
$v_t^d$. Panel~(c) shows the mechanism: the governed reference freezes and
continuously rebases onto the end-effector during the interaction (even
following the human's back-push), then resumes with a cosine ramp from the
rebased position. Both position and velocity references remain continuous
throughout. The certificate quantities are unaffected by construction
(the governed run keeps $E_H\geq E_{H,\min}$ with a peak extraction of
only $0.010$~W, since yielding removes the incentive to fight the human);
the governor is purely a task/recovery policy (claim~6).

\begin{figure}[t]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\figwidth]{figs/fig8_governor.pdf}
  \caption{Reference-governor ablation (blocking scenario, C3; release
  marked at $9$~s). (a)~Tangential velocity: the $0.68$-m/s catch-up burst
  is replaced by a ramp bounded by $v_t^d$. (b)~Normal force: the $24$-N
  release transient is suppressed to $5.1$~N peak. (c)~References: the
  governed $x_g$ freezes and rebases onto the end-effector during the
  interaction instead of tracking the time-indexed $x_d$.}
  \label{fig:governor}
\end{figure}

\subsection{Friction-model sweep}
\label{sec:res-sweep}

Fig.~\ref{fig:sweep} sweeps the predictor coefficient
$\widehat{\mu}\in\{0.20,0.25,0.30,0.35\}$ for C3. Residual depletion
scales monotonically with the model error: $\widehat{\mu}=0.20$ reaches
the floor, $0.25$ consumes roughly half the budget, and
$\widehat{\mu}\geq0.30$ leaves $E_R$ untouched (an over-estimated
$\widehat{\mu}=0.35$ produces $p_\Delta>0$ and charges the ledger). With a
blocking human the first intervention is dominated by the human rows and
is therefore independent of $\widehat{\mu}$, while without a human it
occurs only for the largest mismatch --- and task tracking is flat across
the sweep, confirming that moderate model error costs residual budget, not
task performance. The human-ledger floor is respected at every
$\widehat{\mu}$.

\begin{figure*}[t]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{figs/fig9_sweep.pdf}
  \caption{Friction-predictor sweep for C3 with and without the blocking
  human. (a)~Minimum residual-ledger level: depletion scales with
  $|\mu-\widehat{\mu}|$ and vanishes for $\widehat{\mu}\geq0.30$.
  (b)~First ledger-driven intervention: human-driven ($6.45$~s) when
  present, mismatch-driven ($14.73$~s at $\widehat{\mu}=0.20$) or absent
  otherwise. (c)~Sliding-phase $\mathrm{RMSE}(F_n)$ is insensitive to

\subsection{Summary}
\label{sec:res-summary}

Table~\ref{tab:summary} condenses the comparison. Whole-port accounting
(C1) falsely consumes its budget on predictable friction and destroys the
task it was meant to protect; contact-model subtraction (C2/C3) postpones
intervention until the interaction is unexplained by the task model; the
dedicated human ledger and power row (C3) reduce peak and cumulative
human-directed output by factors of $1.5$ and $8$ relative to C0 while the
residual ledger absorbs model error; two-dimensional task weighting
preserves normal-force regulation $2.9\times$ better than scalar
attenuation under oblique interaction; and the reference governor removes
the post-release burst without touching any certificate quantity.
