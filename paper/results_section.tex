\section{Proof-of-concept results}
\label{sec:results}

Five configurations isolate the main contributions (Table~\ref{tab:roles}).
C0 is the nominal force--motion controller. C1 applies passivity accounting
to the complete measured contact port. C2 instead accounts only for contact
that the task model does not explain. C3 adds a separate human-energy ledger
and human-power limit, with no reference governor. C4 is the same dual-ledger
QP with the reference governor enabled. Thus, C3 and C4 have identical
passivity constraints; only C4 governs the nominal motion reference.

\subsection*{Selective-passivation filter}

\begingroup
\small
\setlength{\abovedisplayskip}{5pt}
\setlength{\belowdisplayskip}{5pt}
\setlength{\abovedisplayshortskip}{3pt}
\setlength{\belowdisplayshortskip}{3pt}

The measured interaction is decomposed into model mismatch and residual
wrenches,
\begin{align}
\mathbf w_\Delta
&=
\mathbf w_T-\widehat{\mathbf w}_T,
&
\mathbf w_R
&=
\mathbf w_{\mathrm{meas}}-\widehat{\mathbf w}_T
=
\mathbf w_H+\mathbf w_\Delta,
\\
p_H
&=
\mathbf w_H^\mathsf{T}\mathbf v_P,
&
p_R
&=
\mathbf w_R^\mathsf{T}\mathbf v_P
=
p_H+p_\Delta .
\end{align}
Here $p_\Delta=\mathbf w_\Delta^\mathsf{T}\mathbf v_P$; $p_i>0$ denotes
energy entering the robot, whereas $p_i<0$ denotes energy delivered by the
robot through port $i$. Each ledger is updated from realized power as
\begin{equation}
E_{i,k+1}
=
\min\!\left(
E_{i,\max},
E_{i,k}+\Delta t\,p_{i,k}^{\mathrm{actual}}
\right),
\qquad i\in\{H,R\}.
\end{equation}
Positive power charges the ledger up to its cap, while negative power
discharges it. The logged raw energy is not lower-clamped, so any floor
violation remains observable.

In the tangential--normal plane, the one-step velocity prediction and robust
lower port power are
\begin{equation}
\mathbf v_{k+1}^{\mathrm{pred}}(\mathbf u)
=
\mathbf A_k\mathbf u+\mathbf b_k,
\qquad
\underline p_i(\mathbf u)
=
\mathbf w_i^\mathsf{T}
\mathbf v_{k+1}^{\mathrm{pred}}(\mathbf u)
-
\|\mathbf w_i\|\,\bar e_v,
\end{equation}
with
\[
\bar e_v=0.025~\mathrm{m/s}.
\]
Under the configured error bound, $\underline p_i$ conservatively
lower-bounds realized port power.

For $\mathbf u=[u_t,u_n]^\mathsf{T}$, C3 solves the task-weighted QP
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\mathbf u^\star
={}&
\arg\min_{\mathbf u}
\frac12
(\mathbf u-\mathbf u_{\mathrm{nom}})^\mathsf{T}
\mathbf W
(\mathbf u-\mathbf u_{\mathrm{nom}})
+
\epsilon_u\|\mathbf u\|^2,
\\
\text{s.t.}\qquad
&E_i+\Delta t\,\underline p_i(\mathbf u)
\ge E_{i,\mathrm{safe}},
\\
&\underline p_i(\mathbf u)
\ge
-k_{\mathrm{cbf}}
\bigl(E_i-E_{i,\mathrm{safe}}\bigr),
\qquad i\in\{H,R\},
\\
&-p_H^{\mathrm{pred}}(\mathbf u)
\le P_H^{\max},
\qquad
\text{plus torque and torque-rate bounds}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
The implemented parameters are
\[
\begin{gathered}
\mathbf W=\operatorname{diag}(1,20),
\qquad
k_{\mathrm{cbf}}=10~\mathrm{s}^{-1},
\qquad
P_H^{\max}=0.10~\mathrm{W},
\\
E_{i,\mathrm{safe}}=E_{i,\min}+10^{-4}~\mathrm{J}.
\end{gathered}
\]
The larger normal weight favors tangential changes and preserves the
normal-force command. The certificate covers only the residual and human
ports: model-consistent task work is intentionally exempted, so global
passivity of the complete actively controlled robot is not claimed.
\endgroup

Across the reported runs, the largest prediction error was $0.0115$~m/s,
below $\bar e_v$; median QP solution time was $24$--$44~\mu$s, compatible
with the 1~kHz control rate.

\FloatBarrier
\subsection{Model-aware accounting preserves useful task work}
\label{sec:res-exemption}

Whole-port passivity cannot distinguish useful contact work from an unsafe
interaction. With an exact contact model and no human, ordinary sliding
friction drains C1's energy ledger and eventually stops the motion
(Fig.~\ref{fig:false-depletion}). C3 subtracts the predicted task-contact
wrench, leaving zero residual in this case. It consequently matches the
nominal controller: $\mathrm{RMSE}(F_n)=0.033$~N and
$\mathrm{RMSE}(v_t)=0.0025$~m/s, with less than $0.02\,\%$ degradation and
no ledger depletion.

\begin{figure}[!htbp]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\figwidth]{figs/fig3_false_depletion.pdf}
  \caption{Exact model, no human. C1 spends its budget on ordinary friction
  and stops sliding. C3 leaves predictable task work unpenalized and follows
  the nominal force and velocity.}
  \label{fig:false-depletion}
\end{figure}

Model error is not ignored; it appears in the residual channel. When the
friction estimate is changed from the true value $\mu\approx0.30$ to
$\widehat\mu=0.20$, the residual ledger falls from $0.30$ to $0.023$~J,
whereas the human ledger stays at $0.05$~J (Fig.~\ref{fig:mismatch}). The
first passivity intervention is delayed from $5.95$~s with whole-port
accounting to $14.59$~s with residual accounting. Thus, the controller
responds to unexplained power rather than to all dissipative task contact.

\begin{figure}[!htbp]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\figwidth]{figs/fig4_mismatch_residual.pdf}
  \caption{Friction-model mismatch without a human. Only the residual ledger
  depletes; the human ledger remains unchanged.}
  \label{fig:mismatch}
\end{figure}

\subsection{A dedicated human port limits transferred energy}
\label{sec:res-human-port}

Under a 3-N blocking force, C0 transfers $0.361$~J to the human port and C2
transfers $0.278$~J. C2 improves on C0 only after its comparatively large
residual budget is exhausted, because the residual combines human input and
model error. C3 separates these effects. Its instantaneous limit holds the
peak human-directed power to $0.0997$~W, and its $0.045$-J usable human
budget limits cumulative output to $0.044$~J. This is an $8.3\times$
reduction relative to C0 and a $6.4\times$ reduction relative to C2. The raw
human ledger remains above its $0.005$-J floor
(Fig.~\ref{fig:human-limiting}).

\begin{figure}[!htbp]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\figwidth]{figs/fig5_human_limiting.pdf}
  \caption{Blocking interaction. C3 respects both the 0.10-W instantaneous
  power limit and the 0.045-J human-energy budget; C0 and C2 do not enforce
  the dedicated human-port limits.}
  \label{fig:human-limiting}
\end{figure}

The two-ledger structure remains interpretable when model error and human
interaction occur together. The human ledger tracks only $p_H$, while the
residual ledger tracks $p_H+p_\Delta$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:combined}). C3 remains
feasible because the smaller human budget acts first; the residual-only C2
exhausts its shared budget in this combined case.

\begin{figure}[!htbp]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\figwidth]{figs/fig6_combined_decomposition.pdf}
  \caption{C3 (no governor) under simultaneous model mismatch and blocking.
  The human and mismatch contributions remain separated, while their sum is
  recorded by the residual ledger.}
  \label{fig:combined}
\end{figure}

The human ledger is also bidirectional. Positive work from a helping human
recharges it up to the $0.08$-J cap; a later blocking interaction spends
that stored energy before approaching the floor (Fig.~\ref{fig:charge}).
This behavior gives the ledger a direct energy interpretation rather than
the meaning of a one-way timeout.

\begin{figure}[!htbp]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\figwidth]{figs/fig7_charge_discharge.pdf}
  \caption{C3 (no governor). A helping interaction charges the human ledger;
  a later blocking interaction uses the stored energy while respecting the
  power limit.}
  \label{fig:charge}
\end{figure}

\FloatBarrier
\subsection{C4 removes the recovery jump}
\label{sec:res-governor}

In C3, the passivity filter can hold the robot while the moving reference
continues to advance. Release then exposes the accumulated position error
and causes a catch-up transient. C4 uses the same QP and safety certificate,
but adds a reference governor upstream. When the human-energy constraint
activates, it smoothly stops the governed reference and fixes it near the
robot. After release, the reference resumes smoothly from this new position
instead of reconnecting to the old time-indexed trajectory.

The fixed anchor retains position stiffness and limits yielding to $1.7$~mm.
The human ledger reaches a minimum of $0.0131$~J, above its certified floor.
The QP therefore provides the safety certificate in both C3 and C4, while
the governor in C4 shapes only the interaction and recovery.

Figure~\ref{fig:governor} compares C3 and C4 over the same interaction. At
release, C3 has $105.5$~mm of reference debt and reaches $24.9$~N command,
$0.681$~m/s velocity, and $19.2$~N normal-force error. C4 reduces these to
$7.5$~mm, $3.00$~N, $0.029$~m/s, and $0.077$~N, respectively.

\begin{widefigure}[!t]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{figs/fig8_governor.pdf}
  \caption{C3 (no governor) versus C4 (with governor) over the complete
  interaction and release. C4 resumes from a fixed reference anchor and
  avoids C3's command and velocity burst. Both use the same passivity QP.}

\end{widefigure}

\section{Conclusion}

The PoC supports three conclusions. First, subtracting predicted task
contact prevents useful work from unnecessarily consuming a passivity
budget. Second, a dedicated human ledger independently bounds power and
energy transferred through the human port. Third, adding the governor to C3
gives C4 a smooth release without changing the certificate. All reported C3
and C4 runs remained feasible and respected their energy floors and
prediction bound. These simulation results assume known human force; physical
experiments and practical force estimation remain future work.
